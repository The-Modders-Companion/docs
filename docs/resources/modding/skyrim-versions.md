!!! abstract "Summary"
     This page explains the differences between the various versions of Skyrim and how they affect modding. I also touch on the Creation Club and the Anniversary Edition DLC.

## Legendary and Special Edition

Skyrim has famously been released and re-released numerous times. The differences between the versions and platforms are relevant for modding.

There are two main versions of Skyrim:

- The original **Skyrim** was released in 2011, followed by its three official DLC (Dawnguard, Hearthfire, Dragonborn). In 2013, the game plus all three DLC was re-released as **Skyrim Legendary Edition (SLE)**. For that reason, the original version of Skyrim is usually referred to as LE or SLE.
- In 2016, a remaster was released called **Skyrim Special Edition (SSE)**. This version also came bundled with all three DLC plus an engine upgrade and the Creation Club (more below). Within a few years, the modding scene had moved over completely to SSE, which is still the version we mod (and play) today.

### The Engine Upgrade

The major difference between LE and SE is related to the **[engine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Game_engine)**, the underlying technical framework upon which the game is built:

- ==**Skyrim LE** ran on a 32bit version of the Creation Engine== which limited the game to 4GB of RAM and generally made it more difficult to build a stable, modded setup.
- ==**Skyrim SE** was given the 64bit engine upgrade== originally built for Fallout 4 which improved the visuals as well as increasing stability. This made it significantly easier to mod Skyrim SE.

!!! bug "Cross-Compatibility"
    Many SLE mods are ^^not^^ compatible out-of-the-box with SSE and need to be ported.

Nowadays, nearly all mods are developed and released for SSE exclusively. The only reason to choose SLE over SSE is performance because SLE still runs better on extremely low-end systems.

## Storefronts

The PC version of Skyrim SE can be bought from several storefronts:

- ✔️ [Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/app/489830/The_Elder_Scrolls_V_Skyrim_Special_Edition/)
- ✔️ [GOG](https://www.gog.com/en/game/the_elder_scrolls_v_skyrim_special_edition)
- ❌ [Epic Games Store](https://store.epicgames.com/en-US/p/skyrim)
- ❌ [XBOX Store / PC Game Pass](https://www.xbox.com/en-GB/games/store/the-elder-scrolls-v-skyrim-special-edition-pc/9P03JGQ4S1GC/0010) (Microsoft)

==For modding, you need either the Steam or GOG version of Skyrim SE.== This is because the **Skyrim Script Extender**, one of the most important mods, only works with those versions of Skyrim. With the EGS or Microsoft versions of the game, you will be extremely limited in your choice of mods.

In addition, it is not possible to use Mod Organizer 2 with the XBOX Store or Game Pass versions of Skyrim.

## Creation Club

A big feature of **Skyrim Special Edition** was the **Creation Club**, a cash shop for mods. After previous attempts to introduce paid mods had failed, Bethesda added the Creation Club to the game to sell so-called 'creations'. Creations are made by Bethesda or by third parties, including many famous modders. 

Despite the difference in terminology, ==there is absolutely no functional difference between creations and mods==. Creations are paid mods with some additional quality assurance.

[Here is a full list of all creations and their contents.](https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Skyrim:Creation_Club)

### Anniversary Edition DLC

In 2021, Skyrim was re-released once again as **Skyrim Anniversary Edition** or **(S)AE**. Notably, SAE is ^^not^^ a 'new' version of Skyrim in the way that SSE was. ==There are no major technical differences between SSE and SAE.== 'Anniversary Edition' simply refers to [the DLC of the same name](https://store.steampowered.com/app/1746860/The_Elder_Scrolls_V_Skyrim_Anniversary_Upgrade/) which is a bundle containing all creations that have so far been released. Even though the terminology seems to suggest otherwise, SAE is the same game version as SSE with some additional content.

Whether you own the **Anniversary Edition DLC** is generally irrelevant for modding. However, a growing number of mods and pre-made setups does require some or all creations.

## Console Ports

Skyrim SE is available for XBOX One and Series X/S, PlayStation 4 and 5, and the Nintendo Switch. These ports all have access to the Creation Club and they are moddable as well.

However, modding on consoles is very basic compared to PC. This is because of the lack of access to the root folder, storage space constraints, the inability to use modding tools, and (in the case of XBOX/PS) [Bethesda.net](http://bethesda.net) being the only source of mods. On consoles, modding really does mean clicking download and install, and hoping for the best.

### SteamDeck

The SteamDeck is in an odd position between console and PC. Since you can access your files and use the Steam version of the game, the modding capabilities of the SteamDeck are quite extensive. Limitations include the Linux-based operating system, storage space constraints, and of course the relatively weak hardware.

While I have no experience with modding on the SteamDeck and can thus give no advice, here are some resources that may help if you are interested in modded Skyrim on the go:

- [A YouTube tutorial by OhNoItsAlexx](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=kpoHTvJmw-o)
- [A written guide on reddit by u/sopholopho](https://www.reddit.com/r/skyrimmods/comments/1044dsa/modding_skyrim_on_the_steam_deck_with/)
- [A Linux installer for Mod Organizer 2](https://github.com/Furglitch/modorganizer2-linux-installer?tab=readme-ov-file)

There are even [Wabbajack lists](https://github.com/Omni-guides/Tuxborn/blob/main/README(temp).md) and [Collections](https://www.nexusmods.com/games/skyrimspecialedition/collections/wfb6lf) for the SteamDeck. See in particular [Omni's excellent guide](https://github.com/Omni-guides/Jackify/wiki/Wabbajack-via-Proton) to using Wabbajack on Proton (for Linux) and installing Wabbajack lists on the SteamDeck.

## Skyrim VR

Skyrim VR – the virtual reality version of Skyrim SE – is available on PC and PlayStation. Once again, there are some caveats for modding.

- The VR port for the **PlayStation** suffers from the same limitations as regular Skyrim SE on consoles and is moddable only in very basic ways.
- The **Steam** version of Skyrim VR is quite moddable, however. You can access all files, modding tools work as usual, and, most importantly, there is a [VR version](https://www.nexusmods.com/skyrimspecialedition/mods/30457) of the Skyrim Script Extender. The Nexus also has *Skyrim VR* and *Skyrim VR Required* tags to find compatible mods.

That said, there are some additional peculiarities and specific mods for the VR version that I am unfamiliar with. Therefore, I can offer no advice or support for modding Skyrim VR.
