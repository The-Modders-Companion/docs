So, you want to get into modding Skyrim. Welcome!

Modding, as you may know, is a rabbit hole of staggering proportions. Most people come into the hobby to mess around with some mods for yet another Skyrim playthrough – only to realise that modding is a bit more complex than anticipated. Mod descriptions are littered with obscure acronyms, seemingly inexplicable issues abound, and reliable information is hard to come by. There are numerous unspoken rules and assumptions that are impossible to parse for newcomers. As a result, many are frustrated and give up.

This article was written to prevent some of that frustration and help you find an entry-point to the hobby. In the following, I will go over basic terminology, explain the differences between custom and pre-made setups, and set realistic expectations by using my patented Cooking Analogy™. In a nutshell, ==there are different ways to acquire a playable modded setup and it's important to choose the one that suits you best==. By the end of the article, you will be able to make an informed decision about how you want to approach modding Skyrim.

## Terminology

Like every area of knowledge, modding has its own terminology and this is usually the first hurdle for newcomers. Because the modding scene is a grassroots movement, its language is developing organically and there is often no consensus on the precise meaning of words. In some cases, a term is specific to the modding community of a particular game and may be used differently by others.

In the following, I will clarify my understanding of terms used in the **Bethesda** (Skyrim, Fallout, Starfield) modding community.

- **Mods** are user-made modifications for a game. Some are standalone and some depend on other mods which must be installed first.
- Members of the modding community are called **Modders**. This term can refer to *people who make mods* and to *people who install mods*. Naturally, these groups overlap. To distinguish between the two, it is helpful to speak of *mod authors* and *mod users* – though keep in mind that most mod authors are also mod users.
- A **Setup** is a selection of mods put together and played by a *mod user*. Setups are often assembled to fit a certain theme (like 'Dark Fantasy' or 'Vanilla Plus'). They range from small (~20 mods) to large (~2.000 mods). You can build a **custom** setup or install a **pre-made** setup (built by someone else).

!!! warning "Mod Packs"
    Mod packs usually refer to individual mod files packaged and distributed together. Due to a tradition of strict sharing permissions, **there are no mod packs** in the Bethesda modding ecosystem. Pre-made setups are shared via manual guides or auto-installers – more on that later.

### What is 'Modding'?

The term 'Modding' is particularly ambiguous because it can refer to the process of *creating new mods* ^^and^^ to the process of *building a setup* from mods created by others.

However, this distinction should not be exaggerated: ==Building a setup requires roughly the same skill set as creating mods==, albeit to a lesser degree. Not coincidentally, many mod authors start out by modifying mods for their personal setup before creating and publishing mods of their own. The transition from building a setup to creating mods is very smooth indeed.

!!! note
    In the context of the *Primer* and the rest of the *Companion*, 'modding' will refer to the process of building a setup.

### What are 'Mod Lists'?

Another highly ambiguous term is **‘mod list’**. Some people refer to guides or their own setups as *mod lists*. Setups installed with the auto-installer Wabbajack are also officially called *mod lists*.

To avoid confusion, it helps to be more specific and distinguish between *setups*, *modding guides*, and *Wabbajack lists*. The term *mod list* should ideally be limited to an actual list of (links to) mods.

## Expectations & Realities

So much for definitions. But what does modding really look like? How difficult is it and how soon can you expect to play a modded setup? 

I’ll try to answer these questions with an analogy.

### The Cooking Analogy

So, modding is a bit like cooking.

You've got a huge variety of ingredients. You select some of those ingredients and put them together in a meal. That meal can be very simple or very complex, and its quality depends on your skill. In the process of preparing the meal, your chosen ingredients interact with each other and create a unique flavour. And in the end, of course, you get to eat.

You can probably see where I'm going with this. Mods are your ingredients and the setup is the meal that you ultimately get to eat – I mean, play. Modding is a skill just like cooking and anyone can learn it, though mastering it takes time. You don't have to be a trained chef to make yourself an omelette for dinner. But if you want to prepare a five-course meal, it really helps to have some experience, a big kitchen, and the right equipment.

In other words:

!!! failure "Rule #2"
    Modding takes time and effort. Building a custom setup requires a solid understanding of file formats and structure as well as of the available tools. 

## Approaches

Here is the most important question at the beginning of your modding journey: **Do you want to mod or do you want to play?** Your answer to that question determines the approach that is right for you.

### Pre-Made Setups

If you're tired and hungry, you're probably best off going to a restaurant. You won't get to fine-tune your meal but you also won't have to make a big effort and, most importantly, you get to eat *now*.

Pre-made setups are like that. ==By using a pre-made setup you trade **customisability** for **ease of use**==. Instead of spending tens or hundreds of hours selecting and tweaking mods, you let someone else do the work – but you also let them make the choices.

There are two ways to acquire a pre-made setup:

- The easiest way is to use **Wabbajack** or Vortex's **Collections** feature. Both are auto-installers that will provide you with a playable modded setup with minimal effort in a short period of time. There is a variety of pre-made setups available, so you are likely to find something that suits your taste. Keep in mind that auto-installers teach you nothing about modding, so you lack the skills to modify the setup once it is installed.
- Alternatively, you can follow a **manual modding guide**. In the course of the guide, you will install a set of mods pre-determined by the author but you will also learn various modding procedures in the process. This can help with building a custom setup later on. Following a manual guide takes longer than running an auto-installer but not nearly as long as building your own setup.

### Custom Setups

You can also cook up your own meal – pick your mods and build a setup of your own. This route takes by far the most time and effort, especially if you have high expectations of the results. The actual time required depends entirely on the number and complexity of your chosen mods, and the level of stability, performance, and consistency you are aiming for.

But while I keep insisting that modding takes effort, it is *not* rocket science. Some basic knowledge goes a long way and if you enjoy tinkering, you will naturally expand your skillset over time. Building a custom is incredibly fun and very rewarding! The biggest roadblock is actually acquiring that basic knowledge – but that's exactly where **The Modder's Companion** comes in.

### Your Choice

And so we cycle back to the initial question: Do you want to mod or do you want to play?

1. If you are looking to mess around with Skyrim mods for an evening, go install [Vortex](https://www.nexusmods.com/vortex), download some mods, and have fun!
2. If you are interested in playing a complex setup but don’t want to pick up a new hobby, check out the [Wabbajack Gallery](https://www.wabbajack.org/gallery?selectedGame=Skyrim%20Special%20Edition) or the [Nexus Collections](https://www.nexusmods.com/games/skyrimspecialedition/collections?collectionStatus=listed&sort=downloads) and install something you like. Don't forget to look for trailers and reviews on YouTube!
3. If you want to get into modding but also play the game reasonably soon, use a manual guide. 
4. If you are determined to build your own (complex) setup, prepare yourself for a long but rewarding journey and lots of trial-and-error. The Modder's Companion is here to assist you on that journey.
